9-Month-Old Gross Motor Skills: What to Expect

Gross motor skills are the abilities required to control the large muscles of the body for activities like sitting, crawling, and eventually walking. It is exciting to watch your baby learn new skills (Source: NIH). At 9 months old, your baby is at a crucial stage of developing these skills as they become more mobile. Babies do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH).

Gross Motor Milestones at 9 Months

At 9 months old, you can expect your baby to be able to:

Sit without support and play with toys (Source: NIH).

Crawl on their hands and knees.

Pull to stand using furniture.

Bear weight on their legs when held upright.

Roll over from back to tummy and tummy to back.

Reach for and grasp toys while sitting.

Begin to cruise along furniture.

How to Support Gross Motor Development

You can encourage your baby&#x27;s gross motor development through play and daily activities:

Provide plenty of tummy time to strengthen neck, arm, and back muscles.

Place toys just out of reach to encourage crawling and reaching.

Practice sitting and playing with toys.

Encourage pulling to stand with a stable surface.

Allow your baby to explore and move freely in a safe environment.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

Babies do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH). However, if you have concerns about your child&#x27;s motor skills, or if they are not meeting typical milestones, talk to your pediatrician. Developmental screening involves giving your child a short test to check for motor, movement, or other developmental delays (Source: NIH). Your provider will check your child&#x27;s motor skills, muscle tone, reflexes, and posture (Source: NIH). Early intervention can make a significant difference.
