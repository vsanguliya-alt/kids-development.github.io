1-Year-Old Speech Delay Signs: A Parent's Guide

Children vary in their development of speech and language skills (Source: NIH). However, there are some key signs that may indicate a speech delay in a 1-year-old. If you have concerns about your child's speech or language development, talk to your child's health care provider (Source: NIH). The earlier a potential delay is identified, the sooner you can get support, which can make a significant difference.

Typical Speech Milestones at 1 Year

By 12 months, most children can:

Say a few single words, such as "mama," "dada," or "ball."

Understand simple words and commands, like "no" or "come here."

Use gestures to communicate, such as pointing, waving, and shaking their head.

Respond to their name and turn to look when you call them.

Babble with a variety of sounds and intonations.

Signs of a Possible Speech Delay

Talk to your pediatrician if your 1-year-old:

Is not saying any single words, like "mama" or "dada."

Does not use gestures, such as pointing, waving, or shaking their head.

Does not respond to their name or seem to understand simple words.

Has not started babbling with different consonant sounds.

Loses language skills they once had, which is a sign that needs immediate attention.

Other Factors to Consider

Sometimes a delay may be caused by hearing loss (Source: NIH). Other times it may be due to a speech or language disorder (Source: NIH). Children who have speech disorders may have trouble making speech sounds correctly (Source: NIH). If your child is not meeting these milestones, trust your instincts and seek an evaluation.

What to Do Next

Schedule a hearing test to rule out hearing loss as a cause.

Talk to your pediatrician about a formal developmental screening.

Ask for a referral to a speech-language pathologist if needed.

When to See a Doctor

You know your child best. If something feels wrong or you are worried about your child's development, don't wait to speak with your pediatrician. Early intervention can make a profound difference in your child's speech and language outcomes.
