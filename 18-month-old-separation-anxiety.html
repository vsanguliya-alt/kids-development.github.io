Separation Anxiety in Your 18-Month-Old

Separation anxiety is a common and normal part of your child&#x27;s development. At 18 months, your child is becoming more aware of their independence but still needs the security of your presence. This can lead to clingy behavior and distress when you leave.

Why Separation Anxiety Happens

Separation anxiety is a sign of healthy attachment. At 18 months, your child:

Has a strong bond with you and sees you as their safe base.

Is developing object permanence, understanding that you exist even when you&#x27;re not there.

Lacks the concept of time, so they may worry that when you leave, you won&#x27;t return.

Is also asserting their independence, which can make them feel vulnerable.

Signs of Separation Anxiety

Your 18-month-old may show separation anxiety through:

Crying or clinging when you try to leave.

Refusing to go to other caregivers.

Waking up at night and calling for you.

Being clingy and wanting to be held frequently.

Showing distress during drop-offs at daycare or with other caregivers.

How to Help Your Child Cope

You can ease your child&#x27;s anxiety with these strategies:

Practice short separations and gradually increase the time you are away.

Establish a consistent goodbye routine, such as a special hug or wave.

Say goodbye with a smile and leave without lingering.

Reassure your child that you will return and always follow through.

Encourage positive interactions with other caregivers.

Read books about separation, such as &quot;The Kissing Hand&quot; or &quot;Llama Llama Misses Mama.&quot;

Provide a comfort object, like a stuffed animal or blanket.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

Separation anxiety is usually a normal phase, but if it interferes with your child&#x27;s daily functioning or lasts beyond toddlerhood, talk to your pediatrician. They can offer guidance and support.
