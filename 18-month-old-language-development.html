18-Month-Old Language Development: What to Expect

Language development is a key milestone in your toddler's growth. The normal development of children aged 1includes language skills, which progress from saying single words to forming sentences (Source: NIH). At 18 months, your child is in the midst of a language explosion, rapidly learning new words and ways to communicate. It's important to remember that toddlers do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH).

Language Milestones at 18 Months

By 18 months, you can expect your child to be:

Saying several single words, typically about 10 to 20 words.

Understanding many more words than they can say.

Following simple, one-step directions, like "give me the toy."

Using gestures such as pointing, waving, and shaking their head to communicate.

Combining two words to form simple phrases, such as "more milk" or "mommy up."

Pointing to a few body parts when named.

Using words to ask for help and to name familiar objects.

Language Milestones at 2 Years

Between 18 months and 2 years, your child will likely:

Increase their vocabulary to about 50 words.

Use two-word sentences more consistently.

Follow two-step directions, like "get your shoes and bring them to me."

Begin to use simple pronouns like "me" and "mine."

Name a few familiar objects in a book.

How to Support Language Development

You can encourage your child's language skills through everyday interactions:

Talk to your child frequently, describing what you are doing and what they are seeing.

Read to your child every day, even if it's just for a few minutes.

Sing songs and recite rhymes to help your child develop an ear for language.

Listen and respond when your child makes sounds or tries to speak.

Name objects and actions during your daily routines.

Play simple games like peek-a-boo and pat-a-cake.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

If you have concerns about your child's language development, especially if they are not reaching typical milestones, talk to your pediatrician. A healthcare professional can assess your child's speech and language skills and recommend early intervention if needed, which can make a significant difference.
