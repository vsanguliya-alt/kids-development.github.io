4-Year-Old Speech Delay Signs: A Parent's Guide

Children vary in their development of speech and language skills (Source: NIH). However, there are some key signs that may indicate a speech delay in a 4-year-old. If you have concerns about your child's speech or language development, talk to your child's health care provider (Source: NIH). The earlier a potential delay is identified, the sooner you can get support, which can make a significant difference.

Typical Speech Milestones at 4 Years

By 4 years old, most children can:

Use sentences of 4 to 5 words and speak clearly.

Understand and follow multi-step directions.

Ask questions like "why?" and "how?"

Have a vocabulary of about 1,000 to 1,500 words.

Use correct grammar most of the time.

Tell stories and talk about things that are not in the present moment.

Signs of a Possible Speech Delay

Talk to your pediatrician if your 4-year-old:

Is difficult to understand for people outside the family.

Is not using sentences of 4 to 5 words.

Has a limited vocabulary.

Does not ask questions.

Does not follow multi-step directions.

Loses language skills they once had, which is a sign that needs immediate attention.

Has a hoarse voice or other voice quality issues.

Has difficulty understanding what others say.

Other Factors to Consider

Sometimes a delay may be caused by hearing loss (Source: NIH). Other times it may be due to a speech or language disorder (Source: NIH). Children who have speech disorders may have trouble making speech sounds correctly (Source: NIH).

What to Do Next

Schedule a hearing test to rule out hearing loss as a cause.

Talk to your pediatrician about a formal developmental screening.

Ask for a referral to a speech-language pathologist if needed.

When to See a Doctor

You know your child best. If something feels wrong or you are worried about your child's development, don't wait to speak with your pediatrician. Early intervention can make a profound difference in your child's speech and language outcomes.
