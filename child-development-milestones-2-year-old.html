Child Development Milestones: Your 2-Year-Old

The normal development of children aged 1includes mastering several areas of skills, and watching your child reach these milestones is an exciting part of parenting (Source: NIH | Toddler Development). At age 2, most children make significant progress in physical, language, cognitive, and social-emotional development .

Physical Milestones

By their second birthday, your child&#x27;s gross motor skills are advancing rapidly. Most 2-year-olds can run and kick a ball . They are also learning to walk up a few stairs (Source: NIH | Toddler Development). Fine motor skills are also developing. They can hold something in one hand while using the other hand, like holding a container and taking the lid off, and they can use a spoon with some success . They can also turn single pages in a book .

Language and Communication Milestones

Language skills grow quickly at this age. By 24 months, a child can often say at least two words together, such as &quot;more milk&quot; (Source: NIH | Toddler Development). They can point to things in a book when asked, and point to at least two body parts . They also use more gestures than waving, such as blowing a kiss or nodding yes (Source: NIH | Toddler Development). They are starting to use words like &quot;I,&quot; &quot;me,&quot; and &quot;you,&quot; and can name common objects and body parts.

Cognitive Milestones

Cognitive development involves learning, thinking, and problem-solving. A 2-year-old can hold something in one hand while using the other, try to use switches, knobs, or buttons on toys, and play with more than one toy at the same time, such as putting toy food on a toy plate . They also begin to engage in simple pretend play, like talking on a toy telephone (Source: NIH | Toddler Development). They can find items hidden under multiple layers and may be able to complete simple puzzles.

Social and Emotional Milestones

By age 2, social skills are becoming more apparent. Most toddlers will show early signs of empathy, such as noticing when another person is upset . They may also look at you to gauge how to react in a new situation (Source: NIH | Toddler Development). They enjoy playing near other children (parallel play) and are beginning to learn about sharing and taking turns.

When to Talk to Your Doctor

It is important to remember that toddlers do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH | Toddler Development). There is a wide range of what is considered &quot;normal&quot; (Source: NIH | Toddler Development). Your child may be ahead in some areas and slightly behind in others (Source: NIH | Toddler Development). However, if you are worried about possible delays, you should talk to your child&#x27;s health care provider (Source: NIH | Toddler Development).
