Child Development Milestones: Your 2-Year-Old

Understanding Your 2-Year-Old's Growth

By age 2, children typically reach key milestones across four main areas: social/emotional, language/communication, cognitive, and physical development . Keep in mind that each child develops at their own pace—some may be advanced in one area and behind in another . If you have concerns, always talk to your child's doctor .

Social and Emotional Milestones

A 2-year-old begins to show empathy and social awareness. They may notice when others are hurt or upset, such as pausing or looking sad when someone is crying . They also look at you to figure out how to react in new situations . While they enjoy being around other children, they typically engage in parallel play (playing next to, but not with, others) and are still learning to share .

Language and Communication Milestones

Language skills grow rapidly at this age. Most children can say at least two words together, like "more milk" . They can point to things in a book when asked and point to at least two body parts . They also use more gestures than just waving, such as blowing a kiss or nodding yes . By 24 months, many children speak about 50 words or more and are understood by caregivers about half the time .

Cognitive Milestones

Cognitive development involves learning and problem-solving. A 2-year-old can hold something in one hand while using the other (e.g., holding a container and taking the lid off) . They try to use switches, knobs, or buttons on toys and play with more than one toy at the same time, like putting toy food on a toy plate . Many also begin simple pretend play, such as talking on a toy telephone .

Physical Milestones

In movement, a 2-year-old can run and kick a ball . They may walk up a few stairs with or without help and can use a spoon with some success . They can also build a tower of 4 or more cubes . Most children at this age need 11 to 14 hours of sleep per day, including naps .

When to Talk to Your Doctor

Contact your child's pediatrician if your 2-year-old:

Is not putting two words together

Does not point to body parts when asked

Is not interested in playing with others

Loses skills they once had

Does not follow simple commands

You know your child best. If you have concerns, act early and talk with your child's doctor .
