Pretend Play in Your 1-Year-Old: A Guide for Parents

Pretend play is a wonderful and important part of your child&#x27;s development. The normal development of children aged 1includes social skills like playing with others, taking turns, and doing fantasy play (Source: NIH). While your 1-year-old is just beginning to explore this type of play, their first steps into imagination are a sign of healthy growth.

The Beginnings of Pretend Play

At 12 months, pretend play is very simple. You might see your child imitate everyday activities, such as pretending to drink from an empty cup or talking on a toy phone. They are copying your actions, which is how they learn about the world. As they approach 18 months, their pretend play becomes more complex. They may feed a doll, pretend to cook, or push a toy car while making a &quot;vroom&quot; sound. They are starting to use one object to represent another and are acting out familiar routines.

Why Pretend Play Matters

Pretend play is not just fun; it is essential for your child&#x27;s development. It helps them develop language skills by using words to describe what they are doing. It also fosters social and emotional skills, as they learn to understand different perspectives and practice empathy. Imaginative play also builds cognitive skills, such as problem-solving and creative thinking.

How to Encourage Pretend Play

You can support your child&#x27;s imagination with simple activities:

Provide simple props: Offer dolls, stuffed animals, toy phones, empty boxes, and kitchen items.

Join the play: Get down on the floor and play with your child. Imitate their actions and add simple comments.

Read books: Reading stories introduces new ideas and characters, which can spark your child&#x27;s imagination.

Sing songs: Nursery rhymes and songs with actions are fun ways to encourage pretend play.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

Toddlers do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH). However, if you are concerned that your child is not engaging in pretend play or is not reaching other developmental milestones, talk to your pediatrician. Your pediatrician can provide guidance and support.
