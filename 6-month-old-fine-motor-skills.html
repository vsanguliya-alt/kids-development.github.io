6-Month-Old Fine Motor Skills: What to Expect

Fine motor skills are the small movements of the hands, fingers, and wrists. At 6 months old, your baby is starting to develop these skills, which are essential for future tasks like feeding themselves and drawing (Source: NIH). Babies do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH), but there are key milestones that are typical for this age.

Fine Motor Milestones at 6 Months

At 6 months, you can expect your baby to be developing the ability to:

Use a raking grasp, meaning they use their whole hand to scoop or rake small objects toward themselves.

Transfer objects from one hand to the other.

Hold a bottle or a small cup with both hands.

Sit with support and use their hands to play with toys.

Reach for and grasp toys that are within their reach.

Shake and bang toys to explore them.

Bring objects to their mouth to explore them.

How to Support Fine Motor Development

You can encourage your baby&#x27;s fine motor skills through everyday play and activities:

Provide toys that are easy to grasp, such as rattles and soft blocks.

Offer finger foods like small pieces of soft fruit or cereal (always supervise closely).

Allow time for tummy time, which helps build the muscles needed for fine motor skills.

Play with toys that make sounds when shaken or squeezed.

Provide a variety of toys with different textures and shapes.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

Babies do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH). However, if you have concerns about your child&#x27;s motor skills, or if they seem to be lagging behind in their development, talk to your pediatrician. Developmental screening involves giving your child a short test to check for motor, movement, or other developmental delays (Source: NIH). Your provider will check your child&#x27;s motor skills, muscle tone, reflexes, and posture (Source: NIH). Early intervention can make a significant difference.
