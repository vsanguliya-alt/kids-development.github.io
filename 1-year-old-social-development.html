1-Year-Old Social Development: What to Expect

Social development is a key part of your child&#x27;s growth. The normal development of children aged 1includes social skills, such as playing with others, taking turns, and doing fantasy play (Source: NIH). At 1 year old, your baby is beginning to move from parallel play to more interactive social experiences. It is important to remember that toddlers do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH).

Social Milestones at 12 Months

At one year old, you can expect your child to be:

Shy or anxious with strangers, which is a healthy sign of attachment.

Enjoying being the center of attention and showing off.

Showing affection for familiar people, like you and other caregivers.

Playing peek-a-boo and other social games.

Watching and imitating the actions of others.

Beginning to understand &quot;mine&quot; and can be possessive of their toys.

Social Milestones at 18 Months

By 18 months, your child&#x27;s social skills become more complex. You may notice them:

Playing alongside other children, but not necessarily with them (parallel play).

Imitating the behaviors of adults and older children.

Showing early signs of empathy, such as looking sad when someone else is crying.

Beginning to assert their independence and may have temper tantrums.

Showing a preference for certain people and toys.

Using simple social gestures like waving &quot;bye-bye.&quot;

How to Support Social Development

You can encourage your child&#x27;s social skills through everyday interactions:

Provide opportunities for playdates and group activities.

Model positive social behavior by sharing, taking turns, and using polite words.

Name and validate their feelings to help them understand their emotions.

Encourage pretend play with simple props.

Read books about friendship, feelings, and social situations.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

If you have concerns about your child&#x27;s social development, especially if they are not showing interest in others or are not meeting milestones, talk to your pediatrician. Routine exams and screenings help you and your kids prevent and treat health problems as well as chart their growth and development (Source: NIH). Early intervention can make a significant difference, so trust your instincts and speak up if something doesn&#x27;t feel right.
