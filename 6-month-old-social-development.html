Social Development in Your 6-Month-Old: What to Expect

Social development is a key part of your baby's growth during the first year. The normal growth of babies includes social skills, such as the ability to play with family members and other children (Source: NIH). At 6 months old, your baby is becoming more social and interactive, showing a preference for familiar people and engaging in back-and-forth interactions. It is important to remember that babies do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH).

Social Milestones at 6 Months

At 6 months old, you can expect your baby to be:

Recognizing familiar faces and showing recognition of people they know.

Showing a preference for you and other primary caregivers.

Smiling and laughing in response to social interactions.

Showing anxiety around strangers, which is a healthy sign of attachment.

Beginning to respond to your emotions, such as becoming upset when you are upset.

Engaging in back-and-forth interactions, such as smiling and making sounds.

Showing interest in other babies and children.

How to Support Social Development

You can encourage your baby's social skills through everyday interactions:

Respond to their cues and provide comfort when they are upset.

Engage in back-and-forth interactions, such as smiling and talking to them.

Provide a safe and predictable environment, which builds a sense of security.

Play with them, such as peek-a-boo, which helps them learn about emotions and social interaction.

Be a role model by expressing your own feelings in a healthy way.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

If you have concerns about your baby's social development, especially if they are consistently withdrawn or not meeting milestones, talk to your pediatrician. Early intervention can make a significant difference.
