6-Month-Old Milestones: A Guide for Parents

The first year of life is full of incredible changes. As your baby approaches 6 months, they will reach many exciting developmental milestones. Remember that babies do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH), so your child may reach some milestones earlier or later than others.

Physical Milestones

At 6 months old, most babies can:

Sit with support and may sit briefly without support (Source: NIH).

Roll over from back to tummy and tummy to back.

Bear weight on their legs when held upright.

Push up on their arms when lying on their stomach.

Hold their head steady and turn it to look at objects.

Use a raking grasp to scoop small objects toward themselves.

Transfer objects from one hand to the other.

Cognitive Milestones

At this age, your baby is developing important thinking skills. They can:

Recognize familiar faces and show recognition of people they know.

Look for partially hidden objects, showing the early development of object permanence.

Explore objects by putting them in their mouth and shaking them.

Copy simple actions, such as babbling back at you.

Show curiosity and interest in their surroundings.

Language Milestones

Language development takes off during the first year. At 6 months, expect your baby to:

Babble and make sounds, such as "ba-ba" or "ga-ga."

Make different sounds to express different feelings.

Respond to their name and turn to look when you call them.

Laugh and chuckle in response to playful interactions.

Social and Emotional Milestones

Your 6-month-old is becoming more social and expressive. They may:

Recognize familiar faces and show recognition of people they know.

Show a preference for you and other primary caregivers.

Smile and laugh in response to social interactions.

Show anxiety around strangers, which is a healthy sign of attachment.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

If you have concerns about your child's development, don't hesitate to talk to your pediatrician. Developmental and behavioral screening tests are available, and your doctor can monitor your child's progress and address any delays early on. Early intervention can make a significant difference.
