1-Year-Old Gross Motor Skills: What to Expect

Gross motor skills are the abilities required to control the large muscles of the body for activities like walking, running, and climbing. It is exciting to watch your toddler learn new skills (Source: NIH). At 1 year old, your child is at a crucial stage of developing these skills as they transition from babyhood to toddlerhood. The normal development of children aged 1includes gross motor skills like walking, running, and climbing (Source: NIH).

Gross Motor Milestones at 12 Months

At one year old, you can expect your child to be able to:

Stand alone for brief periods without support.

Take independent steps and may be walking, though many 1-year-olds are still learning.

Pull to stand and "cruise" along furniture.

Sit down from a standing position with control.

Gross Motor Milestones at 18 Months

By 18 months, your child's gross motor skills become more advanced:

Walking independently and starting to run.

Climbing onto and off furniture without help.

Pulling toys while walking.

Carrying toys while walking.

Walking up and down stairs with assistance.

How to Support Gross Motor Development

You can encourage your child's gross motor development through play and daily activities. Provide plenty of safe opportunities for your child to practice their skills, such as pushing a walking toy. Supervise them during climbing and practice walking together. Allow them to explore their environment safely, which helps build coordination and confidence.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

Babies and toddlers do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH). However, if you have concerns about your child's motor skills, or if they are not meeting typical milestones, talk to your pediatrician. Developmental screening involves giving your child a short test to check for motor, movement, or other developmental delays (Source: NIH). Your provider will check your child's motor skills, muscle tone, reflexes, and posture (Source: NIH). Early intervention can make a significant difference.
