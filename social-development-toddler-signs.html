Fine Motor Skills Development in Your 3-Year-Old

Understanding Fine Motor Skills

Fine motor skills involve the coordination of small muscles in the hands and fingers. These skills are essential for many daily activities and continue to develop rapidly around age three.

Key Milestones for a 3-Year-Old

By their third birthday, most children can perform a range of fine motor tasks. A 3-year-old typically:

Draws a circle when shown how.

Uses scissors to cut paper, though supervision is needed.

Uses a fork to eat.

Strings items together, such as large beads for a bracelet.

Dresses independently with some clothes like loose pants or a jacket.

You may notice your child starting to use one hand as the “doing” hand while the other helps, like holding a bowl while stirring. They are also learning to open containers, fasten large buttons, and brush their teeth with more skill.

When to Talk to Your Doctor

If your child has had lots of practice and is not meeting these milestones, it may be appropriate to seek advice. Contact your child's doctor if your 3-year-old still:

Cannot use a spoon or drink from an open cup.

Cannot remove simple clothing.

Holds a crayon in their fist rather than with their fingers.

Cannot draw lines or a circle.

Remember, children develop at different rates, and the above is an average. Talk with your child's doctor if you have concerns.
