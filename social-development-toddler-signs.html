Social Development in Toddlers: Recognizing Typical Signs

Understanding Social Development

Social development is a key part of your toddler&#x27;s overall growth, involving their ability to interact, express emotions, and relate to others. The normal development of children aged 1includes social skills, which is defined as playing with others, taking turns, and doing fantasy play (Source: NIH | Toddler Development). Watching your toddler learn these new skills is an exciting part of parenthood.

Typical Social Milestones

Social skills evolve significantly between ages 1 and 3. By 15 months, a toddler may begin to show a wider range of emotions and might imitate the actions of others. By 18 months, they might start to play simple pretend games and may show an interest in other children, though they often play alongside them rather than with them, a behavior known as parallel play.

At age 2, your toddler will likely start to become more independent. They may show defiant behavior and have temper tantrums as they test boundaries. They might also start to recognize themselves in a mirror and use words to express their wants. By age 3, a child begins to play more cooperatively with other children. They learn to take turns and may have a &quot;best friend,&quot; demonstrating their growing social awareness and ability to form relationships.

When to Seek Guidance

While children do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH | Toddler Development), there are common warning signs that might indicate a delay. If you are worried about your toddler, you should call your health care provider right away (Source: NIH | Toddler Health). You can learn what the common warning signs are (Source: NIH | Toddler Health). For social development, consider seeking advice if your toddler doesn&#x27;t play simple pretend games, shows no interest in other children, doesn&#x27;t respond to familiar faces, or has a sudden loss of social skills they previously had.

How to Support Social Growth

You can encourage social development by arranging playdates, playing games that involve turn-taking, and talking about emotions. Well-child visits are important to your toddler&#x27;s health, and routine exams and screenings help you and your kids prevent and treat health problems as well as chart their growth and development (Source: NIH | Toddler Health).
