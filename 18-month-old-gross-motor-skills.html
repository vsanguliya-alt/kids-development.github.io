18-Month-Old Gross Motor Skills: What to Expect

Gross motor skills are the abilities required to control the large muscles of the body for activities like walking, running, and climbing. It is exciting to watch your toddler learn new skills (Source: NIH). At 18 months, your child is becoming more mobile and confident in their physical abilities. The normal development of children aged 1includes gross motor skills like walking, running, and climbing (Source: NIH).

Gross Motor Milestones at 18 Months

At 18 months, you can expect your child to be able to:

Walk independently and start to run.

Climb onto and off furniture without help.

Pull toys while walking.

Carry toys while walking.

Walk up and down stairs with assistance.

Kick a ball and throw small objects.

Stand on one foot with support.

How to Support Gross Motor Development

You can encourage your child's gross motor development through play and daily activities:

Provide plenty of safe opportunities for your child to practice their skills, such as pushing a walking toy.

Supervise them during climbing and practice walking together.

Allow them to explore their environment safely, which helps build coordination and confidence.

Engage in active play like rolling a ball back and forth.

Encourage outdoor play with room to run and explore.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

Toddlers do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH). However, if you have concerns about your child's motor skills, or if they are not meeting typical milestones, talk to your pediatrician. Developmental screening involves giving your child a short test to check for motor, movement, or other developmental delays (Source: NIH). Your provider will check your child's motor skills, muscle tone, reflexes, and posture (Source: NIH). Early intervention can make a significant difference.
