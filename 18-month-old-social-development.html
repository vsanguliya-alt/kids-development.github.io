Social Development in Your 18-Month-Old: What to Expect

Social development is a key part of your child&#x27;s growth during the toddler years. The normal development of children aged 1includes social skills, such as playing with others, taking turns, and doing fantasy play (Source: NIH). At 18 months, your child is becoming more aware of others and is starting to engage in more complex social interactions. It is important to remember that toddlers do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH).

Social Milestones at 18 Months

At 18 months, you can expect your child to be:

Playing alongside other children, but not necessarily with them (parallel play).

Imitating the behaviors of adults and older children.

Showing early signs of empathy, such as looking sad when someone else is crying.

Beginning to assert their independence and may have temper tantrums.

Showing a preference for certain people and toys.

Using simple social gestures like waving &quot;bye-bye.&quot;

Enjoying being the center of attention and showing off.

Becoming shy or anxious around strangers, which is a healthy sign of attachment.

How to Support Social Development

You can encourage your child&#x27;s social skills through everyday interactions:

Provide opportunities for playdates and group activities.

Model positive social behavior by sharing, taking turns, and using polite words.

Name and validate their feelings to help them understand their emotions.

Encourage pretend play with simple props.

Read books about friendship, feelings, and social situations.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

If you have concerns about your child&#x27;s social development, especially if they are not showing interest in others or are not meeting milestones, talk to your pediatrician. Early intervention can make a significant difference.
