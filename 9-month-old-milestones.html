9-Month-Old Milestones: A Guide for Parents

The first year of life is full of incredible changes. As your baby approaches 9 months, they will reach many exciting developmental milestones. Remember that babies do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH), so your child may reach some milestones earlier or later than others.

Physical Milestones

At 9 months old, most babies can:

Sit without support and play with toys (Source: NIH).

Crawl on their hands and knees.

Pull to stand using furniture.

Bear weight on their legs when held upright.

Roll over from back to tummy and tummy to back.

Use a pincer grasp, picking up small objects between their thumb and forefinger (Source: NIH).

Transfer objects from one hand to the other.

Cognitive Milestones

At this age, your baby is developing important thinking skills. They can:

Understand object permanence, knowing that objects continue to exist even when they are out of sight.

Look for hidden objects and may try to retrieve them.

Copy simple actions, such as clapping or waving.

Show interest in cause and effect, such as dropping a toy to see what happens.

Recognize familiar people and objects and may show recognition of specific words.

Language Milestones

Language development takes off during the first year. At 9 months, expect your baby to:

Babble with different sounds, such as &quot;ba-ba&quot; and &quot;da-da.&quot;

Make sounds to get your attention.

Respond to their name and turn to look when you call them.

Understand simple words, like &quot;no&quot; and &quot;bye-bye.&quot;

Gesture, such as pointing and waving.

Social and Emotional Milestones

Your 9-month-old is becoming more social and expressive. They may:

Show a preference for you and other primary caregivers.

Show anxiety around strangers, which is a healthy sign of attachment.

Smile and laugh in response to social interactions.

Show interest in other babies and children.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

If you have concerns about your child&#x27;s development, don&#x27;t hesitate to talk to your pediatrician. Developmental and behavioral screening tests are available, and your doctor can monitor your child&#x27;s progress and address any delays early on. Early intervention can make a significant difference.
