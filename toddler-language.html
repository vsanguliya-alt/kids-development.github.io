Toddler Language: A Guide to Speech and Communication Development

It is exciting to watch your toddler learn new skills (Source: NIH). Language development is one of the most fascinating areas of growth. The normal development of children aged 1includes language, from saying single words to forming sentences (Source: NIH). This guide provides an evidence-based overview of what to expect, how to support your child, and when to seek help.

Language Milestones by Age

Toddlers do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH), but there are general milestones that are typical for each age.

12Months

Vocabulary: They say a few single words (e.g., "mama," "dada," "ball").

Understanding: They can understand simple commands and words.

Gestures: They use gestures like pointing, waving, and shaking their head to communicate.

Expression: They use sounds and gestures to get your attention.

18Months

Vocabulary: They have a vocabulary of about 10 to 50 words.

Phrases: They start to combine two words into simple phrases (e.g., "more milk," "mommy up").

Understanding: They follow simple, one-step directions.

Imitation: They imitate words and sounds they hear.

24Months

Vocabulary: They have a vocabulary of several hundred words.

Sentences: They use sentences of 3 to 4 words.

Questions: They start asking questions (e.g., "what's that?").

Conversations: They can engage in simple back-and-forth conversations.

Understanding: They follow two-step directions.

How to Support Your Toddler's Language Development

Talk to your child frequently: Narrate what you are doing and describe what they see and hear.

Read to your child every day: Reading is one of the most powerful ways to build language skills.

Sing songs and recite rhymes: The rhythm and repetition are excellent for language learning.

Listen and respond: When your child speaks, respond with interest and expand on what they say.

Name objects and actions: Label things around the house and during your daily routines.

Play games: Simple games like "I Spy" encourage description and vocabulary.

Engage in pretend play: This encourages language use in different contexts and scenarios.

When to Be Concerned

Children vary in their development of speech and language skills (Source: NIH). However, there are some warning signs that may indicate a delay.

Speak to your child's health care provider if your toddler:

Is not saying any words by 18 months.

Is not using two-word phrases by 24 months.

Does not follow simple directions.

Is difficult to understand for people outside the family.

Loses language skills they once had.

Has a hoarse voice or other voice quality issues.

Speech and Language Disorders

Other times it may be due to a speech or language disorder (Source: NIH). Children with language disorders might have trouble understanding what they hear or forming sentences. Early intervention is key to helping children with speech or language delays.

The Role of Play in Language Development

Social play is an important context for language development. Playing with others, taking turns, and doing fantasy play all build communication skills (Source: NIH). Play provides a natural and engaging environment for children to practice their language.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I call the doctor about my toddler's language development?

Call your doctor if your toddler is not meeting typical milestones, such as not saying any words by 18 months, not using two-word phrases by 24 months, or if you have any concerns about their speech or understanding.

How long does it take for a toddler to learn to talk?

Language development is a gradual process. Most children start with a few words around 12 months and combine words into sentences between 18 and 24 months. The process is different for every child.

What home remedies can help a toddler's language development?

The best "remedy" is interaction: talk, read, and sing with your child. The more language your child hears, the more they will learn. There are no medical remedies for speech development.

What are the signs of a language delay in a toddler?

Signs include not saying any words by 18 months, not using two-word phrases by 24 months, not understanding simple directions, and losing language skills they once had.

Does screen time affect toddler language development?

Yes, excessive screen time can interfere with the face-to-face interaction that is essential for language development. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends limiting screen time for toddlers.

Can a toddler have a language delay without other issues?

Yes, a language delay can be an isolated issue. However, it's important to have your child evaluated to rule out other possible causes, such as hearing loss.

How does a toddler's language development relate to their social development?

Language and social development are closely linked. Children use language to interact with others, form friendships, and express their feelings and needs.
