6-Month-Old Development: When to Worry

As a parent, it can be difficult to know if your baby's development is on track. Babies do not develop at the same rate, and children grow and mature at very different rates. However, there are some signs that may indicate a need for further evaluation.

Red Flags in Development

Talk to your pediatrician if your 6-month-old:

Is not sitting with support or sitting independently.

Is not rolling over from back to tummy or tummy to back.

Is not bearing weight on their legs when held upright.

Is not pushing up on their arms when lying on their stomach.

Is not holding their head steady and turning it to look at objects.

Is not reaching for and grasping toys.

Is not babbling or making sounds.

Is not responding to their name or turning to look when you call them.

Is not laughing or chuckling in response to playful interactions.

Is not making eye contact or showing interest in social interaction.

Has poor muscle tone (feels stiff or floppy).

Loses skills they once had, which is a sign that needs immediate attention.

What to Do If You're Concerned

Trust your instincts. You know your baby best. If something feels wrong, don't hesitate to speak up.

Talk to your pediatrician. Schedule an appointment to discuss your concerns. They can perform a developmental screening and refer you to specialists if needed.

Seek early intervention. The sooner a potential delay is identified, the sooner you can get support, which can make a significant difference.

When to See a Doctor

If you have any concerns about your baby's development or behavior, don't wait to speak with your pediatrician. Early intervention can make a profound difference in your child's long-term outcomes.
