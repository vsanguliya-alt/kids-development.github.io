18-Month-Old Development: When to Worry

As a parent, it can be difficult to know if your child&#x27;s development is on track. While toddlers do not develop at the same rate, there are some signs that may indicate a need for further evaluation. If you have concerns about your child&#x27;s development, don&#x27;t hesitate to talk to your pediatrician.

Red Flags in Development

Talk to your pediatrician if your 18-month-old:

Is not walking independently or seems to have lost motor skills they once had.

Is not saying any words.

Does not use gestures, such as pointing, waving, or shaking their head.

Does not respond to their name or seem to understand simple words.

Has not started babbling with different consonant sounds.

Loses language or social skills they once had, which is a sign that needs immediate attention.

Has poor eye contact or seems uninterested in social interaction.

Is not reaching for or grasping objects.

Has a persistent lack of interest in their environment.

Does not engage in pretend play.

Has extreme difficulty with changes in routine.

What to Do If You&#x27;re Concerned

Trust your instincts. You know your child best. If something feels wrong, don&#x27;t hesitate to speak up.

Talk to your pediatrician. Schedule an appointment to discuss your concerns. They can perform a developmental screening and refer you to specialists if needed.

Seek early intervention. The sooner a potential delay is identified, the sooner you can get support, which can make a significant difference.

When to See a Doctor

If you have any concerns about your child&#x27;s development or behavior, don&#x27;t wait to speak with your pediatrician. Early intervention can make a profound difference in your child&#x27;s long-term outcomes.
