9-Month-Old Language Development: What to Expect

Language development is one of the most exciting areas of growth to watch in your 9-month-old. The normal growth of babies includes language skills, such as starting to make sounds, learning some words, and understanding what people say (Source: NIH). At 9 months old, your baby is becoming more interactive and expressive, using sounds and gestures to communicate. It's important to remember that babies do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH).

Language Milestones at 9 Months

At 9 months old, you can expect your baby to be:

Babbling with different sounds, such as "ba-ba" and "da-da."

Making sounds to get your attention.

Responding to their name and turning to look when you call them.

Understanding simple words, like "no" and "bye-bye."

Gesturing such as pointing and waving.

Imitating sounds and simple actions.

Using voice to express feelings like happiness or frustration.

How to Support Language Development

You can encourage your baby's language skills through everyday interactions:

Talk to your baby frequently, narrating what you are doing.

Read simple board books and point to pictures.

Sing songs and recite rhymes to help your baby develop an ear for language.

Listen and respond when your baby makes sounds.

Name objects and actions during your daily routines.

Play simple games like peek-a-boo and pat-a-cake.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

Babies do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH). However, if you have concerns about your baby's language development, especially if they are not making sounds or responding to you, talk to your pediatrician. A healthcare professional can assess your baby's speech and language skills and recommend early intervention if needed, which can make a significant difference.
