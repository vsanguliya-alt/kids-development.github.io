Toddler Tantrums: A Guide for Parents

If you are the parent of a toddler, you have likely experienced the sudden, powerful storm of a tantrum. It is exciting to watch your toddler learn new skills, but this stage of development also comes with big emotions that can feel overwhelming for both you and your child. This guide provides evidence-based information on understanding, managing, and preventing toddler tantrums.

What Are Tantrums?

Tantrums are emotional outbursts that are a normal part of a toddler's development. They are your child's way of expressing frustration, anger, or disappointment when they cannot have what they want, cannot do what they want to do, or do not have the words to express their feelings. Tantrums can involve crying, screaming, kicking, hitting, and even holding their breath.

Why Do Tantrums Happen?

Understanding why tantrums happen can help you respond with more patience and empathy.

Developmental Milestones

Toddlers are learning to be independent and are discovering their own will. When they cannot do something they want to do, they may feel a surge of frustration. This is a normal part of developing a sense of self.

Limited Communication

Toddlers often do not yet have the vocabulary to express their complex emotions. When they cannot communicate their needs or wants, they may resort to a tantrum.

Basic Needs

Tantrums are often triggered by simple things like being tired, hungry, overstimulated, or feeling ill. A toddler who is past their nap time or hungry is much more likely to have a meltdown.

How to Handle a Tantrum

When a tantrum is happening, your goal is to keep your child safe while helping them calm down.

Stay Calm

Your toddler looks to you for cues on how to react. If you remain calm, it can help them de-escalate. Take a deep breath and lower your voice.

Ensure Safety

If your child is hitting or throwing things, gently but firmly remove them from the situation. Make sure they are not in a place where they can hurt themselves.

Use a Calm Voice

Get down to your child's level and speak in a soothing, simple tone. For example, "I see you are upset. It is okay to be angry, but it is not okay to hit."

Acknowledge Their Feelings

Validating your toddler's feelings can help them feel understood. You might say, "I know you are frustrated because you wanted the toy."

Wait It Out

Sometimes, the best thing to do is simply let the tantrum run its course. Stay nearby and offer comfort when they are ready to receive it.

How to Prevent Tantrums

While you cannot prevent every tantrum, you can reduce their frequency and intensity.

Establish a Routine

Toddlers thrive on predictability. A consistent daily routine for meals, naps, and bedtime can help prevent tantrums triggered by tiredness or hunger.

Offer Choices

Give your child a sense of control by offering simple choices. For example, "Do you want the red cup or the blue cup?" This can help prevent power struggles.

Praise Good Behavior

Catch your child being good. Offer specific praise, such as, "I loved how you shared your toy with your friend."

Create a Safe, Boring Place

If a tantrum occurs, a quiet, safe area with few distractions can help them calm down.

When to Seek Professional Help

If you are worried about your toddler, call your health care provider right away. In rare cases, frequent, severe, or very long tantrums can be a sign of a more serious problem. Talk to your child's doctor if:

Tantrums happen often and are very intense.

Your child hurts themselves or others during tantrums.

The tantrums seem to be getting worse.

Your child has trouble calming down.

Tantrums are affecting your child's daily life or your family life.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I call the doctor about my toddler's tantrums?

Call your doctor if your child's tantrums are very frequent, severe, cause them to hurt themselves or others, or if you are concerned about your child's development or behavior.

How long do toddler tantrums usually last?

The average tantrum lasts between 3 and 5 minutes, but they can sometimes last longer. The duration often decreases as a child's language and self-regulation skills improve.

What home remedies can help with toddler tantrums?

There are no "home remedies" for tantrums, but you can prevent them by ensuring your child gets enough sleep, food, and exercise. During a tantrum, staying calm and using a gentle voice can help.

Is it normal for a toddler to hold their breath during a tantrum?

Yes, some children will hold their breath during a tantrum. This is usually harmless and they will start breathing again on their own. If your child faints, lays them on their side; they will usually wake up within a minute.

Can a lack of sleep cause tantrums?

Yes, sleep is essential for emotional regulation. A tired toddler is much more likely to have a tantrum. Ensuring your child gets the recommended 11hours of sleep per day can help reduce tantrums.

What are the signs of a tantrum?

Signs include crying, screaming, kicking, hitting, throwing things, or holding their breath. Tantrums usually happen when a child is frustrated, angry, or upset.

How can I discipline my toddler during a tantrum?

Discipline during a tantrum should focus on safety and calming down, not on punishment. After your child has calmed down, you can talk about what happened in simple terms.
