1-Year-Old Language Development: What to Expect

Language development is one of the most exciting areas of growth to watch in your 1-year-old. The normal development of children aged 1includes language skills, from saying single words to eventually forming sentences (Source: NIH). It's important to remember that toddlers do not develop at the same rate, and there is a wide range of what is considered normal (Source: NIH).

Language Milestones at 12 Months

At one year old, you can expect your child to be:

Babbling in a way that sounds like real speech, with changes in tone and rhythm.

Saying a few single words like "mama," "dada," or "ball."

Understanding simple words and commands, such as "no" or "come here."

Gesturing to communicate, such as pointing, waving, and shaking their head.

Responding to their name and turning to look when you call them.

Language Milestones at 18 Months

By 18 months, your child's language skills will expand significantly. You may notice them:

Using more words, typically about 10 to 20 words.

Understanding many more words than they can say.

Following simple, one-step directions, like "give me the toy."

Using gestures and sounds to get your attention.

Starting to use two-word phrases, such as "more milk."

Language Milestones at 2 to 3 Years

By age 2, most children are using two-word sentences and have a vocabulary of about 50 words. They can follow two-step directions and are beginning to use simple grammar. By age 3, their vocabulary has grown to several hundred words, and they can speak in three- to four-word sentences.

How to Support Language Development

You can encourage your child's language skills through everyday interactions:

Talk to your child frequently, describing what you are doing and what they are seeing.

Read to your child every day, even if it's just for a few minutes.

Sing songs and recite rhymes to help your child develop an ear for language.

Listen and respond when your child makes sounds or tries to speak.

Name objects and actions during your daily routines.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

Children vary in their development of speech and language skills (Source: NIH). However, if you have concerns about your child's language development, especially if they are not reaching typical milestones, talk to your pediatrician. A healthcare professional can assess your child's speech and language skills and recommend early intervention if needed, which can make a significant difference.
