4-Year-Old Development: When to Worry

As a parent, it can be difficult to know if your child's development is on track. The normal development of children includes several areas: gross motor, fine motor, sensory, language, and social skills (Source: NIH). It's important to remember that children do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH), and children grow and mature at very different rates. However, there are some signs that may indicate a need for further evaluation.

Red Flags in Development

Talk to your pediatrician if your 4-year-old:

Is not walking, running, or climbing with confidence.

Is not using sentences of 4 to 5 words.

Has difficulty understanding or following multi-step directions.

Is difficult to understand for people outside the family.

Does not show interest in playing with others.

Has poor eye contact or seems uninterested in social interaction.

Loses language or social skills they once had, which is a sign that needs immediate attention.

Has extreme difficulty with changes in routine.

Is not engaging in pretend play.

Has a limited vocabulary.

Does not ask questions.

What to Do If You're Concerned

Trust your instincts. You know your child best. If something feels wrong, don't hesitate to speak up.

Talk to your pediatrician. Schedule an appointment to discuss your concerns. They can perform a developmental screening and refer you to specialists if needed.

Seek early intervention. The sooner a potential delay is identified, the sooner you can get support, which can make a significant difference.

When to See a Doctor

If you have any concerns about your child's development or behavior, don't wait to speak with your pediatrician. Early intervention can make a profound difference in your child's long-term outcomes.
