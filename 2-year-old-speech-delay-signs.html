2-Year-Old Speech Delay Signs: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

Children vary in their development of speech and language skills (Source: NIH). However, there are some key signs that may indicate a speech delay in a 2-year-old. If you have concerns about your child&#x27;s speech or language development, talk to your child&#x27;s health care provider (Source: NIH). The earlier a potential delay is identified, the sooner you can get support, which can make a significant difference.

Typical Speech Milestones at 2 Years

By 2 years old, most children can:

Use simple sentences of 2 to 4 words, such as &quot;more milk&quot; or &quot;my toy&quot; (Source: NIH).

Have a vocabulary of about 50 words.

Understand many more words than they can say.

Follow two-step directions, like &quot;get your shoes and bring them to me.&quot;

Name a few familiar objects in a book.

Ask simple questions, such as &quot;what&#x27;s that?&quot;

Signs of a Possible Speech Delay

Talk to your pediatrician if your 2-year-old:

Is not using two-word phrases. For example, not saying &quot;more milk&quot; or &quot;mommy up.&quot;

Has a limited vocabulary of fewer than 50 words.

Does not follow simple directions, like &quot;give me the toy.&quot;

Does not use gestures such as pointing, waving, or shaking their head.

Loses language skills they once had, which is a sign that needs immediate attention.

Has difficulty understanding simple questions or commands.

Is not imitating sounds or words.

Other Factors to Consider

Sometimes a delay may be caused by hearing loss (Source: NIH). Other times it may be due to a speech or language disorder (Source: NIH). Children who have speech disorders may have trouble making speech sounds correctly (Source: NIH).

What to Do Next

Schedule a hearing test to rule out hearing loss as a cause.

Talk to your pediatrician about a formal developmental screening.

Ask for a referral to a speech-language pathologist if needed.

When to See a Doctor

You know your child best. If something feels wrong or you are worried about your child&#x27;s development, don&#x27;t wait to speak with your pediatrician. Early intervention can make a profound difference in your child&#x27;s speech and language outcomes.
