Separation Anxiety in Your 9-Month-Old: A Guide for Parents

Separation anxiety is a common and normal part of your baby's emotional development. At 9 months old, your baby is beginning to understand that they are separate from you and may start to show distress when you leave. The normal growth of babies includes the ability to play with family members and other children, but they also develop strong attachments (Source: NIH).

Why Separation Anxiety Happens

Separation anxiety is a sign of healthy attachment. At 9 months old, your baby:

Has a strong bond with you and sees you as their safe base.

Is developing object permanence, understanding that you exist even when you're not there.

Lacks the concept of time, so they may worry that when you leave, you won't return.

Signs of Separation Anxiety

Your 9-month-old may show separation anxiety through:

Crying or clinging when you try to leave the room.

Becoming upset when you hand them to someone else.

Showing a strong preference for you over other caregivers.

Waking up at night and calling for you.

Being clingy and wanting to be held frequently.

How to Help Your Baby Cope

You can ease your baby's anxiety with these strategies:

Practice short separations and gradually increase the time you are away.

Establish a consistent goodbye routine, such as a special hug or wave.

Say goodbye with a smile and leave without lingering.

Reassure your baby that you will return and always follow through.

Encourage positive interactions with other caregivers.

Provide a comfort object, like a stuffed animal or blanket.

When to Seek Professional Help

Separation anxiety is usually a normal phase, but if it interferes with your baby's daily functioning or lasts beyond toddlerhood, talk to your pediatrician. They can offer guidance and support. In some cases, a doctor or mental health specialist can provide a comprehensive evaluation.
