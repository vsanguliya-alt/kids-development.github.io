1-Year-Old Milestones: A Guide for Parents

Mastering new skills such as how to walk, talk, and use the potty are developmental milestones (Source: NIH). The first year of life is full of incredible changes. As your baby approaches their first birthday and enters toddlerhood, they will reach many exciting developmental milestones. Remember that babies do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH), so your child may reach some milestones earlier or later than others.

Physical Milestones

By 12 months, most 1-year-olds are very active. You can expect them to be able to:

Pull up to stand and "cruise" along furniture.

Stand alone for brief periods.

Take independent steps and may be walking, though many 1-year-olds are still learning.

Sit down from a standing position with control.

By 18 months, your child may be walking independently, starting to run, and climbing onto furniture. They will also be developing better hand-eye coordination, such as feeding themselves with a spoon.

Cognitive Milestones

At 1 year old, your child is developing important thinking skills. They are beginning to understand object permanence—the concept that objects continue to exist even when they cannot be seen. They are also starting to solve simple problems and copy your actions.

By 18 months, your child can follow simple one-step directions, recognize themselves in a mirror, and imitate your activities. Their memory is also improving.

Language Milestones

Language development takes off during the toddler years. At 12 months, expect your child to be babbling with expression, saying a few single words, understanding simple commands, and using gestures like pointing and waving.

By 18 months, their vocabulary should expand to about 10words, and they may even begin to form two-word phrases.

Social and Emotional Milestones

Your 1-year-old is becoming more social and aware of their own emotions. At 12 months, they show attachment to you, may be cautious around strangers, and begin to show affection. They also enjoy play and humor.

By 18 months, they may show more independence, express a wider range of emotions, and begin to show early signs of empathy.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

If you have concerns about your child's development, don't hesitate to talk to your pediatrician. Developmental screening involves giving your child a short test to check for motor, movement, or other developmental delays. Your provider can check your child's motor skills, muscle tone, reflexes, and posture. Early intervention can make a significant difference, so trust your instincts and speak up if something doesn't feel right.
