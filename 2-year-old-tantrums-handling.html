When Should You Worry About a 2-Year-Old's Tantrums?

Tantrums are a normal part of a 2-year-old's development. They happen because toddlers feel big emotions like frustration and disappointment, but their brains are not yet developed enough to regulate those feelings . While most tantrums are typical, certain signs indicate that a child may need additional support.

What Is Normal at 2 Years?

At age 2, it is common for a child to have 1 to 2 tantrums per day that last 2 to 15 minutes . These outbursts are usually triggered by identifiable causes such as hunger, tiredness, transitions, or not getting what they want . A normal tantrum resolves with comfort or distraction, and the frequency and intensity should gradually decrease as the child's language and coping skills improve .

Red Flags: When to Worry

While tantrums are typical, you should be concerned and consult your pediatrician if you notice any of these signs :

1. Tantrums Are Frequent and Prolonged:

Frequency: Tantrums happen more than 5 times per day or are occurring nearly every day .

Duration: Tantrums regularly last more than 20 to 25 minutes .

2. Tantrums Are Aggressive or Destructive:

The child injures themselves (e.g., head-banging hard enough to bruise, biting, or scratching until they bleed) .

The child hits, kicks, or bites others consistently .

The child destroys property during an outburst .

3. Tantrums Are Accompanied by Other Concerning Symptoms:

The child holds their breath and faints during a tantrum .

The child has frequent headaches, stomachaches, or nightmares .

The child shows regression of skills they once had, such as bedwetting after being toilet trained .

4. Tantrums Persist Beyond the Typical Age:

Tantrums continue frequently past the age of 4 without decreasing .

If your child exhibits any of these red flags, talk to your pediatrician. They can assess whether the behavior falls within the normal spectrum or points to a deeper issue and can provide strategies for managing behavior at home .

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor about my 2-year-old's tantrums?

Call your pediatrician if tantrums are frequent (more than 5 times a day), prolonged (lasting more than 20 minutes), aggressive (causing injury to self or others), or are accompanied by symptoms like nightmares, headaches, or regression of skills .

Q: How long do normal tantrums last?

Most toddler temper tantrums last for a few minutes up to 15 minutes. Children typically recover and move on with their day afterward .

Q: What home remedies help with handling tantrums?

Maintain a consistent routine, try to identify and avoid triggers, offer simple choices, distract your child with a new activity before a tantrum starts, and use a calm, friendly tone when making requests . Avoid overreacting and reward good behavior with praise .

Q: Is it normal for a 2-year-old to have tantrums every day?

Yes, it is normal for 2-year-olds to have 1 to 2 tantrums per day . These are often triggered by frustration, tiredness, or transitions. However, if they are occurring much more frequently, it may be a red flag .

Q: Does teething affect a 2-year-old's behavior?

Teething can cause discomfort and may lead to temporary changes in a child's mood. However, it should not cause severe, prolonged, or aggressive tantrums. If you are concerned about your child's behavior, talk to your pediatrician.

Q: When should I go to the ER for a child having a tantrum?

Go to the ER if your child is injuring themselves severely (e.g., head banging hard enough to cause injury, or holding their breath until they lose consciousness), has a seizure, or if you are concerned about your own ability to stay calm and keep them safe .
