6-Month-Old Language Development: What to Expect

Language development is one of the most exciting areas of growth to watch in your 6-month-old. The normal growth of babies includes language skills, such as starting to make sounds, learning some words, and understanding what people say (Source: NIH). At 6 months old, your baby is beginning to understand the rhythm and patterns of speech and is starting to experiment with sounds. It&#x27;s important to remember that babies do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH).

Language Milestones at 6 Months

At 6 months old, you can expect your baby to be:

Babbling and making sounds, such as &quot;ba-ba&quot; or &quot;ga-ga.&quot;

Making different sounds to express different feelings.

Responding to their name and turning to look when you call them.

Recognizing familiar voices and showing recognition when you speak.

Laughing and chuckling in response to playful interactions.

Gurgling and cooing in social situations.

How to Support Language Development

You can encourage your baby&#x27;s language skills through everyday interactions:

Talk to your baby frequently, narrating what you are doing.

Read simple board books and point to pictures.

Sing songs and recite rhymes to help your baby develop an ear for language.

Listen and respond when your baby makes sounds.

Name objects and actions during your daily routines.

Play simple games like peek-a-boo and pat-a-cake.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

Babies do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH). However, if you have concerns about your baby&#x27;s language development, especially if they are not making sounds or responding to you, talk to your pediatrician. A healthcare professional can assess your baby&#x27;s speech and language skills and recommend early intervention if needed, which can make a significant difference.
