6-Month-Old Speech Delay Signs: A Parent&#x27;s Guide

Children vary in their development of speech and language skills (Source: NIH). However, there are some key signs that may indicate a speech or language delay in a 6-month-old. If you have concerns about your child&#x27;s speech or language development, talk to your child&#x27;s health care provider (Source: NIH). The earlier a potential delay is identified, the sooner you can get support, which can make a significant difference.

Typical Speech Milestones at 6 Months

By 6 months old, most children can:

Babble and make sounds, such as &quot;ba-ba&quot; or &quot;ga-ga.&quot;

Make different sounds to express different feelings.

Respond to their name and turn to look when you call them.

Laugh and chuckle in response to playful interactions.

Gurgle and coo in social situations.

Make sounds when looking at toys or people.

Signs of a Possible Speech Delay

Talk to your pediatrician if your 6-month-old:

Is not babbling or making sounds.

Does not respond to their name or turn to look when you call them.

Does not laugh or chuckle in response to playful interactions.

Does not make eye contact or show interest in social interaction.

Does not use sounds to express different feelings.

Other Factors to Consider

Sometimes a delay may be caused by hearing loss (Source: NIH). Children start learning speech and language long before they talk (Source: NIH). Other times it may be due to a speech or language disorder (Source: NIH).

What to Do Next

Schedule a hearing test to rule out hearing loss as a cause.

Talk to your pediatrician about a formal developmental screening.

Ask for a referral to a speech-language pathologist if needed.

When to See a Doctor

You know your child best. If something feels wrong or you are worried about your child&#x27;s development, don&#x27;t wait to speak with your pediatrician. Early intervention can make a profound difference in your child&#x27;s speech and language outcomes.
