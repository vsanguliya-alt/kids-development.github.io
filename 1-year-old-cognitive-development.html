1-Year-Old Cognitive Development: What to Expect

Cognitive development refers to the skills and processes involved in thinking, learning, and problem-solving. For a 1-year-old, this is a period of rapid cognitive growth as they learn to understand and interact with the world around them. While toddlers do not develop at the same rate, there are key milestones that are typical for this age .

Cognitive Milestones at 12 Months

At 1 year old, your child is beginning to understand object permanence—the concept that objects continue to exist even when they cannot be seen. They are also starting to show curiosity and an understanding of how things work, such as dropping objects to see what happens.

They are also beginning to solve simple problems, like figuring out how to reach a toy that is just out of reach. Their memory is developing, and they may recognize familiar people and objects, and remember simple routines.

Cognitive Milestones at 18 Months

An 18-month-old&#x27;s cognitive abilities become more sophisticated. They are able to follow simple one-step requests, like &quot;give me the ball.&quot; They may also recognize themselves in a mirror, which is a sign of growing self-awareness.

They may also demonstrate imitation skills by copying everyday activities, such as sweeping or talking on a phone, as they learn by watching and mimicking the actions of others.

How to Support Cognitive Development

Provide a stimulating environment with safe, age-appropriate toys.

Engage in serve-and-return interactions, like responding to a babble with a smile or a word. This helps your child learn about communication and cause-and-effect.

Talk to your child frequently, describing what you are doing together.

Read to your child daily to build language and comprehension skills.

Allow your child to explore safely and independently.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

You know your child best. If you have concerns about your child&#x27;s development or behavior, don&#x27;t wait to speak with your pediatrician. Routine exams and screenings help you and your kids prevent and treat health problems as well as chart their growth and development . Trust your instincts—if something doesn&#x27;t feel right, it&#x27;s always best to get it checked.
