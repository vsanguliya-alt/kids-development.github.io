Social Development in Your 4-Year-Old: What to Expect

Social development is a key part of your child&#x27;s growth during the preschool years. The normal development of children aged 1includes social skills, such as playing with others, taking turns, and doing fantasy play (Source: NIH). At 4 years old, your child is becoming more aware of others and is starting to engage in more complex social interactions. It is important to remember that children do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH).

Social Milestones at 4 Years

At 4 years old, you can expect your child to be:

Engaging in cooperative play with other children.

Taking turns and sharing, though it may still be difficult.

Participating in simple group activities.

Using language to communicate their needs and wants.

Showing more complex emotions like jealousy and pride.

Developing friendships and showing a preference for certain playmates.

Imitating the behaviors of adults and older children.

Showing empathy, such as offering comfort to a crying friend.

How to Support Social Development

You can encourage your child&#x27;s social skills through everyday interactions:

Provide opportunities for playdates and group activities.

Model positive social behavior by sharing, taking turns, and using polite words.

Name and validate their feelings to help them understand their emotions.

Encourage pretend play with simple props.

Read books about friendship, feelings, and social situations.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

If you have concerns about your child&#x27;s social development, especially if they are not showing interest in others or are not meeting milestones, talk to your pediatrician. Routine exams and screenings help you and your kids prevent and treat health problems as well as chart their growth and development (Source: NIH). Early intervention can make a significant difference.
