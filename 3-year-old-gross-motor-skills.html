3-Year-Old Gross Motor Skills: What to Expect

Gross motor skills are the abilities required to control the large muscles of the body for activities like walking, running, and climbing. It is exciting to watch your toddler learn new skills (Source: NIH). At 3 years old, your child is becoming more mobile and confident in their physical abilities. The normal development of children aged 1includes gross motor skills like walking, running, and climbing (Source: NIH).

Gross Motor Milestones at 3 Years

At 3 years old, you can expect your child to be able to:

Run with more speed and agility.

Climb confidently on playground equipment.

Jump off low steps and jump in place.

Pedal a tricycle.

Walk backwards and balance on one foot for a few seconds.

Kick and throw a ball with more coordination.

Hop on one foot.

How to Support Gross Motor Development

You can encourage your child&#x27;s gross motor development through play and daily activities:

Provide plenty of safe opportunities for your child to practice their skills.

Supervise them during climbing and active play.

Allow them to explore their environment safely, which helps build coordination and confidence.

Engage in active play, such as throwing and catching a ball.

Encourage outdoor play with room to run and explore.

Enroll them in classes like gymnastics or dance.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

Toddlers do not develop at the same rate (Source: NIH). However, if you have concerns about your child&#x27;s motor skills, or if they are not meeting typical milestones, talk to your pediatrician. Developmental screening involves giving your child a short test to check for motor, movement, or other developmental delays (Source: NIH). Your provider will check your child&#x27;s motor skills, muscle tone, reflexes, and posture (Source: NIH). Early intervention can make a significant difference.
