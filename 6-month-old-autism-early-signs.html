Autism Early Signs in Your 6-Month-Old: A Guide for Parents

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a neurological and developmental condition that begins early in childhood and lasts throughout a person&#x27;s life . At 6 months old, it is important to monitor your baby&#x27;s development and look for early signs, even though a formal diagnosis is rarely made at this age. Most parents of children with ASD suspect that something is wrong by the time the child is 18 months old . However, recognizing potential &quot;red flags&quot; early is the first step toward getting your child the support they may need. Acting early can lead to better results for your child.

Early Signs to Watch For

By 6 months, most babies are beginning to socialize and interact. The following &quot;red flags&quot; may indicate a child is at risk for ASD and needs an evaluation:

Limited or no eye contact – Your baby may not look at you when you are interacting with them.

Lack of social smiling – They may not smile back at you when you smile.

Reduced or no facial expressions – Their face may remain expressionless during social interactions.

Lack of joyful expressions – They may not smile or show happiness when interacting with people.

Limited babbling – They may not make sounds or babble as expected.

Does not respond to their name – They may not turn or look when you call their name.

Not reaching for objects – They may show less interest in exploring their environment.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

Babies do not develop at the same rate, but it is always best to trust your instincts. If you have concerns about your baby&#x27;s development, or if you notice any of the signs above, do not wait to speak with your pediatrician. Your pediatrician can perform a developmental screening and guide you on next steps. The sooner a potential delay is identified, the sooner you can get support, which can make a significant difference.
