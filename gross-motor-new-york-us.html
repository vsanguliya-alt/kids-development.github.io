Gross Motor Skills in New York: A Guide for Parents

Gross motor skills involve the large muscles of the body and are essential for movements like rolling, sitting, crawling, walking, running, and climbing . These foundational skills are not just about physical fitness—they are closely linked to brain development, self-regulation, and school readiness . Understanding typical milestones and the resources available in New York can help you support your child's development.

What Are Gross Motor Milestones?

Children develop at different rates, but New York's early learning standards, the New York State Prekindergarten Learning Standards (NYS PKS), outline key milestones :

Birth to 18 months: Rolling, sitting, pulling to stand, and beginning to walk. Sensory-motor exploration through reaching and grasping is also a focus .

18 to 36 months: Running, climbing, and jumping. Balance and coordination improve significantly .

3 to 5 years: Hopping, skipping, galloping, catching, and throwing. Children gain more control over their movements .

When to Seek Help

Pediatricians and early childhood experts recommend seeking support if you have concerns about your child's motor development. According to the NYC Department of Health and Mental Hygiene, early identification of developmental delays plays a crucial role in helping children reach their full potential. If your child is not meeting expected milestones, or shows a loss of skills they once had, it is a reason to seek an evaluation.

Resources for Gross Motor Support in New York

New York City and State offer extensive support for families concerned about their child's gross motor development.

New York City Early Intervention Program: For children from birth to age 3, this program provides free developmental evaluations and, if eligible, therapeutic services like physical therapy . You can call 311 to refer your child for an evaluation . Services are often provided in the child's home or daycare setting .

New York State Prekindergarten Learning Standards (NYS PKS): This framework, used by licensed childcare programs, outlines developmental milestones and supports educators in tracking a child's progress in gross motor skills from birth through kindergarten entry .

Pediatric Physical Therapy: New York has a wealth of pediatric physical therapists who specialize in gross motor development. Many offer in-home, in-school, or clinic-based services, including play-based therapy approaches that feel natural and engaging for children . Some providers in the city include:

EMpower: Offers in-home pediatric physical and occupational therapy with a focus on play-based strategies .

BLOOM OT: Provides holistic occupational, physical, and speech therapy with a neurodiverse-affirming approach .

Motor Skills Matter: Specializes in pediatric physical therapy evaluation and treatment for infants, toddlers, and preschoolers, addressing delays in gross motor skills, balance, and coordination .

NK Progress, Inc.: A New York State-approved provider of Early Intervention services, including physical therapy for gross-motor skills .

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor about my child's gross motor skills?

Call your pediatrician if you are concerned about your child's development, or if they are not meeting expected gross motor milestones for their age, such as rolling, sitting, or walking. In NYC, you can also call 311 to connect with the Early Intervention Program for children under 3 .

Q: How can I help my child develop gross motor skills?

You can support your child's gross motor skills through play-based activities at home. Encourage tummy time, provide opportunities for crawling and walking, and engage in active play like running, jumping, and climbing. Outdoor play is particularly important .

Q: What activities are best for gross motor development?

Activities that support gross motor development include tummy time, crawling games, walking practice, running, climbing, jumping, hopping, skipping, and throwing and catching balls .

Q: When should I go to the ER for a motor skill issue?

Go to the ER immediately if your child has a sudden loss of motor skills, especially if accompanied by other symptoms like a fever, severe headache, difficulty breathing, or a seizure. For non-emergency concerns, contact your pediatrician or the Early Intervention Program in NYC.
