9-Month-Old Speech Delay Signs: A Parent's Guide

Children vary in their development of speech and language skills (Source: NIH). However, there are some key signs that may indicate a speech or language delay in a 9-month-old. If you have concerns about your child's speech or language development, talk to your child's health care provider (Source: NIH). The earlier a potential delay is identified, the sooner you can get support, which can make a significant difference.

Typical Speech Milestones at 9 Months

By 9 months old, most children can:

Babble with different sounds, such as "ba-ba" and "da-da."

Make sounds to get your attention.

Respond to their name and turn to look when you call them.

Understand simple words, like "no" and "bye-bye."

Gesture, such as pointing and waving.

Imitate sounds and simple actions.

Signs of a Possible Speech Delay

Talk to your pediatrician if your 9-month-old:

Is not babbling or making sounds.

Does not respond to their name or turn to look when you call them.

Does not understand simple words like "no" or "bye-bye."

Does not gesture, such as pointing or waving.

Does not imitate sounds or simple actions.

Other Factors to Consider

Sometimes a delay may be caused by hearing loss (Source: NIH). Children start learning speech and language long before they talk (Source: NIH). Other times it may be due to a speech or language disorder (Source: NIH).

What to Do Next

Schedule a hearing test to rule out hearing loss as a cause.

Talk to your pediatrician about a formal developmental screening.

Ask for a referral to a speech-language pathologist if needed.

When to See a Doctor

You know your child best. If something feels wrong or you are worried about your child's development, don't wait to speak with your pediatrician. Early intervention can make a profound difference in your child's speech and language outcomes.
