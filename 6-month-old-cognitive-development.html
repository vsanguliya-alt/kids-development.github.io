Cognitive Development in Your 6-Month-Old: What to Expect

Cognitive development refers to the skills and processes involved in thinking, learning, problem-solving, and memory. At 6 months old, your baby is becoming more aware of their environment and is learning to interact with the world in more complex ways.

Cognitive Milestones at 6 Months

At 6 months old, your baby can typically:

Recognize familiar faces and show recognition of people they know.

Look for partially hidden objects, showing the early development of object permanence.

Reach for and grasp toys and objects within their reach.

Explore objects by putting them in their mouth and shaking them.

Copy simple actions, such as babbling back at you.

Show curiosity and interest in their surroundings.

Respond to their name and turn to look when you call them.

Smile and laugh in response to social interactions.

How to Support Cognitive Development

You can encourage your baby's cognitive growth through everyday play and interactions:

Talk to your baby frequently, narrating what you are doing.

Read simple board books and point to pictures.

Sing songs and play games like "peek-a-boo."

Provide safe toys that are interesting to touch, shake, and explore.

Allow your baby time to explore and practice reaching for objects.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

Babies do not develop at the same rate, but it is always best to trust your instincts. If you have concerns about your baby's development, talk to your pediatrician. Developmental and behavioral screening tests are available, and your doctor can monitor your child's progress and address any delays early on.
