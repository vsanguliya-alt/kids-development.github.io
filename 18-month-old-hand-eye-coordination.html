When Should You Worry About 18-Month-Old Hand-Eye Coordination?

Hand-eye coordination, or fine motor skills, involves the small muscles in the hands and fingers working together with vision . At 18 months, a wide range of development is considered normal, with many children starting to master new and exciting skills.

What is Normal at 18 Months?

It's important to remember that children develop at different rates, and the following are average milestones, not strict deadlines . At around 18 months, many toddlers can:

Scribble spontaneously with a crayon or pencil .

Build a tower using 3 to 4 blocks .

Turn several pages of a book at a time .

Feed themselves partially with a spoon .

Imitate drawing a straight line or circle .

Use a pincer grip (thumb and first finger) to pick up small objects, a skill that should be established by this age .

Red Flags: When to be Concerned

While there is a wide range of normal, certain signs may indicate a delay in hand-eye coordination or an underlying vision problem that warrants a discussion with your pediatrician.

Hand Preference Too Early: Children should not become obviously right- or left-handed until they are older than 18 months. If your child strongly favors one hand over the other at this age, it can be a red flag .

Not Using Both Hands Equally: If your child is under 18 months and is not using both hands equally to reach for and manipulate objects, this is a concern .

Difficulty with Vision: Watch for signs that your child may not be seeing well, such as not being able to follow objects with their eyes, frequently rubbing their eyes, or tilting their head when looking at things . This could be a sign of "lazy eye" (amblyopia) or another vision problem .

Problems with Coordination: While some clumsiness is normal, difficulty with activities like catching, throwing, or frequent tripping or falling can be signs of a problem .

Regression of Skills: Any loss of previously acquired skills, such as no longer scribbling or building with blocks, is a significant red flag that requires immediate medical evaluation .

When to Call the Doctor

If you notice any of these red flags, or if you are simply worried about your child's development, do not hesitate to contact your pediatrician. Early identification and intervention can make a significant difference.

Trust your instincts: You know your child best. If you have a persistent feeling that something is not right, bring it up with your doctor.

Prepare for the visit: Before your appointment, make a note of the specific skills you are concerned about and any examples that worry you, to help your pediatrician assess the situation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When should I call the doctor about my 18-month-old's hand-eye coordination?

Call your pediatrician if your child is not using both hands equally, strongly favors one hand, shows signs of vision problems (like not tracking objects), or you are worried about their development . Any loss of skills is also a red flag .

Q: How can I help improve my 18-month-old's hand-eye coordination?

Offer a variety of age-appropriate toys and activities that encourage fine motor skills, such as building blocks, shape sorters, chunky puzzles, crayons for scribbling, and finger foods .

Q: Is it normal for an 18-month-old to be clumsy?

Some clumsiness is normal at this age as children master new motor skills. However, if your child seems to be falling or tripping significantly more than other children their age, it's worth discussing with your pediatrician .

Q: What can cause a delay in hand-eye coordination?

Delays can sometimes be linked to vision problems, which are the most common issues to develop at this age . Other causes can include general developmental delays or neurological conditions.

Q: When should an 18-month-old have an eye test?

Your child's eyes are routinely checked by a pediatrician during well-child visits. If you have specific concerns about their vision, you can ask for a more detailed screening or a referral to a pediatric ophthalmologist .
