Emotional Development in Your 6-Month-Old: What to Expect

Emotional development is a crucial part of your baby's growth during the first year. At 6 months old, your baby is becoming more social and expressive, showing a wider range of emotions and attachments.

Key Emotional Milestones at 6 Months

During this stage, you can expect to see your baby:

Recognizing familiar faces and showing recognition of people they know.

Showing a preference for you and other primary caregivers.

Smiling and laughing in response to social interactions.

Expressing joy, anger, and sadness more clearly.

Showing anxiety around strangers, which is a healthy sign of attachment.

Beginning to respond to your emotions, such as becoming upset when you are upset.

Cooing and babbling to communicate their needs and feelings.

How to Support Emotional Development

You can help your baby develop emotional skills through everyday interactions:

Respond to their cues and provide comfort when they are upset.

Engage in back-and-forth interactions, such as smiling and talking to them.

Provide a safe and predictable environment, which builds a sense of security.

Play with them, such as peek-a-boo, which helps them learn about emotions and social interaction.

Be a role model by expressing your own feelings in a healthy way.

When to Talk to Your Pediatrician

Babies do not develop at the same rate, but if you have concerns about your baby's emotional development, especially if they are consistently withdrawn or not meeting milestones, talk to your pediatrician. Your pediatrician can monitor your child's progress and address any concerns early on.
